# THE TRINKADOOS
## Layout & Illustration Brief — First Wave

**Compiled:** 2026-08-31
**Companion to:** the manuscript file and the series bible.

---

## PURPOSE

This is the pagination and illustration document. For each of the ten books it gives the **page/spread structure** and the **art brief for every page**, paired with the text that sits on it.

Nothing in the ART blocks is printed. They are direction for the illustrator and the layout designer.

**These briefs are a starting point, not a specification.** They are expected to be challenged, modified, and improved by whoever does the pagination. Where a brief is load-bearing — where the story breaks if the picture doesn't do its job — it is marked **LOAD-BEARING** below.

---

## FORMAT SPEC (all ten books)

- **Trim:** 8.5 × 8.5 in, square
- **Extent:** 32 pages
- **Structure:** pp. 1–2 front matter · **p. 3 opener** · **pp. 4–31 fourteen spreads** · **p. 32 closer**
- **Bleed:** full-bleed illustration assumed throughout — build to trim + 0.125 in
- **Interior:** one art layout serves paperback, hardcover, and ebook

---

## LOAD-BEARING ART — read this before anything else

Some beats exist only in the picture. If these are changed, the text stops working:

- **Book 8, Spread 3** — ordinary fog beyond the bridge, **zero words**. This is the setup that makes Spread 12's "Not ordinary fog" mean something. If it is cut, Book 8's turn has no baseline.
- **Book 4, Spread 10** — the ART note must say **dark band**, never "shadow." Naming the shadow spoils the reveal one spread early.
- **Book 7, Spread 2** — the wall clue goes deliberately unread. Nobody sees it, including the reader.
- **Outfits and packs** are always physically retrieved, worn, removed, and stored. They never fade in or materialize. This applies to every panel in every book.
- **Zinumi never speaks.** No speech bubbles, no lettering, ever.

# BOOK 1 — The Lantern Tree Went Dark

**Spotlight:** Bram · **Emotional core:** — · **765 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> The park was theirs on Saturdays.

> Their families took the same three tables by the water, and the four of them knew every root, every rock, and every good climbing branch.

> Today they were racing to the mossy log when Sivi stopped so fast she almost fell over.


**ART:** Establish all four children in ordinary clothes, ordinary daylight, big natural park — towering trees, rocky rise in the far background. No magic visible yet. Sivi mid-skid, the others piling up behind her. Canon-critical: picnic tables with families at them are visible in the middle distance, unremarkable and out of earshot. Adults are never in focus and never have faces the reader learns.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> Something small was floating between the trees.

> It was watching them.

> "Do you see it?" Bram whispered.

> "I see it," said Nico.

> "I see it too," said Tessa.

> The little creature spun in the air and made a sound like three tiny bells.


**ART:** First look at Zinumi — small, luminous, clearly *aware* of them. The text no longer describes her, so the picture carries everything: wings, face, scale, mischief. Must read delighted, never frightening. Four faces lit from below by her glow; no child looking elsewhere.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> She darted away, then stopped. Then darted again.

> "She wants us to follow," said Sivi, already running.

> She led them past the picnic tables, past the big rocks, to an old, old tree leaning against a hill.

> Between the roots and the stone was a gap that had never been there before.


**ART:** Page-turn payoff — the hidden entrance. Huge ancient tree pressed against a rocky rise. The opening should look like something an adult's eye would slide right past. Children small against it.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> Bram went first, because Bram always went first when somebody might be scared.

> Inside, the dark was not dark at all. Moss glowed green. Roots glowed gold. Tiny lights drifted past their noses.

> The fairy landed on Tessa's shoulder.

> "Zinumi," Tessa decided.

> The fairy chimed twice, which meant yes.


**ART:** The Glowing Passage. This is the first big wonder spread — green, gold, lavender, warm white. Zinumi perched on Tessa's shoulder, the two of them nose to nose. Bram slightly ahead of the group, one arm back toward the others.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> The tunnel opened into a room.

> Somebody had built it a long time ago, and left the light on, and never come back.

> It was still warm.

> "It's a clubhouse," Sivi breathed. "It's *our* clubhouse."

> Zinumi tugged Nico's sleeve and pointed deeper in.


**ART:** The Hidden Clubhouse Chamber — cozy, warm, part cave and part old cabin. The prose no longer lists the furnishings, so the art must: worn benches, shelves, lanterns made of curled leaves. Empty of the children's own treasures for now. Zinumi pulling Nico toward a darker opening at the back: the page turn.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> The next room was older. Quieter.

> Four backpacks rested in four hollows in the stone.

> One brown. One cream. One green. One purple.

> Nobody touched them.

> Then, one at a time, they began to glow.


**ART:** First reveal of the Trinkadoos Pack Chamber and the four packs at rest. Hero-object treatment — each pack in its own stone niche, clearly designed, clearly waiting. Resting state on the left of the spread, first glow beginning on the right. Children hanging back, hands to themselves.


### SPREAD 6 (pp. 14–15)

**TEXT:**

> The brown one warmed like a hug, and Bram picked it up.

> The cream one lit before Tessa even touched it.

> The green one hummed low for Nico.

> The purple one lifted right off the stone and bumped into Sivi, who laughed out loud.

> "They picked us," said Tessa.


**ART:** Each pack choosing its child — four simultaneous moments in one spread. Sivi's is the comedy beat: her pack in the air, nudging her chest. Establish the Bear / Unicorn / Turtle / Butterfly visual language on the packs themselves without making them literal animals.


### SPREAD 7 (pp. 16–17)

**TEXT:**

> Nico opened his first.

> He looked inside for a while.

> "It's deeper than it is," he said.

> Sivi put her whole arm in, up to the shoulder. Then her head.

> Inside the packs, folded and waiting, were clothes. Four sets. Exactly four.


**ART:** The dimensional-storage reveal — canon-critical. The interior must read as impossibly deep and softly lit, not as a hole. Nico peering in with his careful face; Sivi head-first with only her legs showing. This is the spread that has to sell the whole magic system.


### SPREAD 8 (pp. 18–19)

**TEXT:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings.

> They put them on, sleeve by sleeve, buckle by buckle.

> Everything fit.

> Nobody said anything about how everything fit.

> Then Sivi turned around, and her wings unfolded, and knocked Bram straight over.


**ART:** First full look at all four adventure forms — bear ears, golden horn, turtle shell, violet wings. The text no longer names any of it, so this spread is the entire costume reveal. Canon-critical: they are physically dressing themselves, mid-motion, clothes half on — NOT a magical materialization. End on Bram flat on his back and Sivi delighted.


### SPREAD 9 (pp. 20–21)

**TEXT:**

> A picture slid across the stone wall.

> A great tree, standing in the dark, with its lights going out one by one.

> Zinumi pressed both hands to the wall. Her glow went pale.

> "Somebody needs help," said Bram.

> "Then we're going," said Sivi.

> The room folded around them like light closing a hand.


**ART:** Two beats. Left: the clue on the wall — simple, dark, urgent, no explanation. Right: the transport — light folding around the four of them. Keep Zinumi's alarm readable purely through posture and dimmed glow.


### SPREAD 10 (pp. 22–23)

**TEXT:**

> They came out into night.

> Not dark night. *Glowing* night.

> The stream below them shone. The leaves had light in their edges. Small creatures carried lanterns on their backs.

> And in the middle of all of it stood an enormous tree, black as a shadow, with nothing shining in it at all.


**ART:** The wonder spread — biggest, most cinematic image in the book. Everything luminous except the Lantern Tree, which is a dead silhouette at the center. Wonder first, worry second. The four children tiny at the bottom edge, looking up.


### SPREAD 11 (pp. 24–25)

**TEXT:**

> "It's broken," said Sivi.

> Bram was not so sure. Every time a spark started deep in the trunk, the tree pulled it back in.

> Nico crouched by a root. "Look. It's torn."

> Tessa put her hands out. Pale gold light spread across the ground, and a path of roots lit up under the leaves — a hurt one, and a whole one.


**ART:** Tessa's first REVEAL. Lavender-white and soft gold. Show two root-paths becoming visible under the forest floor: one broken, one intact. Bram apart from the group, watching the trunk, noticing what nobody else has.


### SPREAD 12 (pp. 26–27)

**TEXT:**

> The whole path crossed a gully, and it was shaking.

> Nico went still. He breathed. Green light came, and the roots slowed, and held.

> Sivi flew up for the glowing seeds caught high in the branches.

> Then the storm came back — one last push of wind and sparks.

> Bram threw his arms wide, and warm amber light spread over his friends like two great paws.


**ART:** Three powers in one spread, staged left to right: Nico's green-gold STEADY on the crossing; Sivi small and violet high in the branches; Bram's amber HOLD as the payoff on the right-hand page. His light should read as protective, never as a weapon.


### SPREAD 13 (pp. 28–29)

**TEXT:**

> They packed the torn roots with glowing seeds until the light ran through them again.

> But the tree stayed dark.

> Bram put his hand on the bark.

> "You're scared," said Bram.

> "Me too."

> The tree did not answer.

> "We fixed it," said Bram. "Try one little one."


**ART:** Quiet spread. Pull the light and the scale down — one small boy, one enormous dark trunk, his hand flat against it. The other three hanging back, giving him room. No magic effects on this spread at all. Canon-critical: Bram is frightened here too — he is admitting something, not comforting from a position of calm.


### SPREAD 14 (pp. 30–31)

**TEXT:**

> One small light came on.

> Then another.

> Then light ran up the trunk and out along every branch and fell into the forest like rain, and the whole world turned gold and green and blue at once.

> The Lantern Tree was shining.


**ART:** The visual payoff of the book. Full relight, root to crown, light spilling outward across the entire forest. Every lantern-creature, stream, and leaf blazing. Children silhouetted against it, arms up. This is the spread the book gets bought for.


### PAGE 32 — CLOSER

**TEXT:**

> Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

> Outside, the park looked exactly the same. The picnic was still warm.

> Nobody moved for a second.

> Then all four of them started talking at once.

> **END OF BOOK 1 MANUSCRIPT**


**ART:** Return ritual, compressed — children back in ordinary clothes, four packs resting in their niches, glow gone. Canon-critical: outfits are physically stored, not faded; the art must show the undressing the text now only names. Final image: the four of them outside in daylight, mid-explosion of talking, Zinumi just visible in the leaves behind them.

# BOOK 2 — The Baby Dragon of Cloudstone

**Spotlight:** Sivi · **Emotional core:** encouragement is not the same as pressure · **530 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> They came back the next Saturday, and the Saturday after that.

> The cave was theirs now. Tessa had put drawings on the wall. Nico had lined up his good rocks.

> Zinumi was asleep in a teacup.


**ART:** The clubhouse chamber, now visibly lived in — first sign that the children's things accumulate here across the series. Ordinary clothes. Zinumi curled inside a chipped teacup somebody brought from home. Cozy, funny, safe.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> Then Zinumi sat straight up.

> In the next room, one pack was glowing.

> The purple one.

> "That's mine," said Sivi. "That's MINE."


**ART:** Two beats. Left: Zinumi bolt upright in the teacup, glow gone bright. Right: through the doorway, only Sivi's butterfly pack lit in its niche — the other three dark. Establish that packs can wake one at a time.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> They knew what to do this time.

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> On the wall, a small shape sat alone on a small stone, high above the clouds.

> "Somebody's stuck," said Bram.


**ART:** Prep compressed — four children mid-dressing, quick and practiced, nothing like the wonder of Book 1. Right-hand page: the clue on the stone wall, simple and lonely. Canon-critical: outfits still physically pulled out and put on.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> The sky went on forever.

> This was Cloudstone. Great stones floated in it like islands, trailing mist, drifting slowly past one another.

> Sivi opened her wings and made a sound like a kettle.


**ART:** The wonder spread. Enormous luminous sky, Cloudstones at many depths, atmospheric perspective. The prose no longer describes the color, so the palette is entirely the art's job — pink beneath the clouds, gold above. Children small and thrilled on the largest stone; Sivi already airborne.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> On the smallest stone of all sat a baby dragon.

> It had been there a long time.

> "Pim," Tessa decided.

> "You can't just name it," said Sivi.

> "I just did."

> Between its stone and theirs was a very wide gap.


**ART:** Baby-schema cuteness carrying the page — the text describes none of it now. Round body, wings far too big, four tiny horns, deeply unimpressed face. Show the gap clearly: the problem must be readable in one look. Tessa already crouched and committed; Sivi objecting from the air.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> "Oh, that's easy!" said Sivi.

> She jumped. She flapped. She glided.

> "See? Jump, flap, glide. Jump, flap, glide!"


**ART:** Sivi demonstrating with total confidence, in three small motion panels or one sweeping arc. Joyful. The dragon in the corner of the frame, already leaning back.


### SPREAD 6 (pp. 14–15)

**TEXT:**

> The dragon backed away.

> So Sivi did it faster. And bigger. And higher.

> "Like THIS!"

> The dragon put its wings over its head.


**ART:** Sivi filling the sky, delighted, oblivious. The dragon small and shrinking in the bottom corner. The page has to make the reader feel the mismatch before Sivi does.


### SPREAD 7 (pp. 16–17)

**TEXT:**

> The others tried too.

> Bram spread warm amber light across the gap so nothing could fall.

> Tessa held out her hands, and the air lit up gold with soft, slow currents.

> Nico watched a long time. "The same wind comes back every four," he said.

> The dragon did not move.


**ART:** Three helpers, three approaches, one stubborn dragon. Bram's HOLD as a glowing floor under the gap; Tessa's REVEAL making invisible air currents visible; Nico simply counting. Everything the children can do is on the page — and it isn't working.


### SPREAD 8 (pp. 18–19)

**TEXT:**

> "Why won't you just—" said Sivi.

> And then she stopped.


**ART:** Sivi mid-sentence, arms out, frustrated. Second panel or facing page: her face changing. This is the quietest moment so far and needs white space around it.


### SPREAD 9 (pp. 20–21)

**TEXT:**

> She landed. She folded her wings all the way in.

> She sat down next to the dragon and did not say anything at all.

> It took a long time.


**ART:** Two small figures side by side on a small stone in an enormous sky. Wings folded — visually the opposite of every previous Sivi spread. No magic on this page.


### SPREAD 10 (pp. 22–23)

**TEXT:**

> The dragon turned one wing toward her.

> There was a scrape along the edge of it.

> It looked down, over the side, all the way down.

> "Oh," said Sivi. "You already tried."


**ART:** Close and gentle. The scraped wing. Then the dragon's eye tracking downward into the drop below the stone. The fall should feel long. This is the reveal — the dragon isn't refusing, it's remembering.


### SPREAD 11 (pp. 24–25)

**TEXT:**

> "Okay," said Sivi. "Then we're not going to make you go."

> She looked at the others.

> "We're going to make it the safest jump anybody ever jumped."


**ART:** The turn. Sivi still sitting, but talking back over her shoulder to the other three, who are already moving. The dragon watching her, not the gap.


### SPREAD 12 (pp. 26–27)

**TEXT:**

> Bram spread his amber light wide underneath.

> Nico went still, and the drifting stone stopped drifting.

> Tessa found the softest current and lit it gold, all the way across.

> And Sivi flew up beside the dragon, close enough to touch — and didn't.

> "Whenever you want," she said.


**ART:** All four working, staged low to high: Bram's light beneath, Nico steadying the stone, Tessa's gold path across the gap, Sivi hovering alongside with her hands deliberately open and empty. Canon-critical: she does not pull, carry, or push it.


### SPREAD 13 (pp. 28–29)

**TEXT:**

> The dragon looked at the gold path.

> It leaned.

> It jumped.

> And it dropped.


**ART:** Page-turn cliff. End the spread on the drop — the dragon falling, wings not open yet, amber light glowing far below. Frightening for exactly one page turn, never longer.


### SPREAD 14 (pp. 30–31)

**TEXT:**

> Then its wings snapped open and caught the whole enormous sky.

> It went up.

> It went up past Bram and past Nico and past Tessa, and it did a loop, and it did another one, and Sivi flew after it laughing so hard she nearly fell out of the air.


**ART:** The payoff spread. Dragon and Sivi tumbling upward together through miles of glowing cloud, the other three cheering from the stone below. Biggest, brightest, most joyful image in the book.


### PAGE 32 — CLOSER

**TEXT:**

> Before they left, the dragon pushed something into Sivi's hand. A small pale scale, the color of a cloud.

> Sivi found a pocket she hadn't noticed before, and put the scale inside, and closed it up carefully.

> Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

> "Same time next week," she said.

> **END OF BOOK 2 MANUSCRIPT**


**ART:** Return ritual, compressed — ordinary clothes back on, outfits stored, four packs at rest. Canon-critical: show the scale going *into* the pack. This is the series' first keepsake and must be visibly stored, not just received. Consider staging the closer to echo Book 1's final image so the ritual reads as a series signature.

# BOOK 3 — The Forest That Lost Its Colors

**Spotlight:** Nico · **Emotional core:** the first explanation is not always the right one · **572 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> Bram had brought exactly one apple for four people.

> Zinumi got the first bite, because she asked nicely.

> She asked nicely by sitting on it.


**ART:** The clubhouse chamber. One apple, four children, and a fairy sitting squarely on top of it looking pleased with herself. Warm and funny and about nothing. Do not foreshadow anything.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> The apple never got finished.

> The green pack was glowing.

> Zinumi shot out of the doorway so fast she left a trail of sparks behind her, and came back, and shot out again.


**ART:** Nico's turtle pack alone in its niche, lit; the other three dark. Zinumi's panic is entirely physical — she never speaks. The apple abandoned mid-bite on the floor.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> On the wall was a forest.

> All the color was running out of the bottom of it, like water out of a tub.


**ART:** Prep compressed to the ritual line — the art carries the dressing. Right-hand page: the clue on the stone. Show color literally draining downward and pooling out of frame. This is the book's whole mystery in one image.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> They came out under trees as tall as weather.

> Enormous flowers. Curling ferns. Birds the size of Bram.

> Every single bit of it was gray.


**ART:** The wonder spread, and it works by subtraction. Everything must be spectacular in shape and scale and completely drained of color — the reader should feel how magnificent this place is *supposed* to be. One exception hidden somewhere for sharp-eyed readers: a small wet stone with color in it.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> "SOMEBODY TOOK THEM!" shouted a small creature with enormous ears.

> "It was the diggers!"

> "It was the birds!"

> "It was *that* one! Look at him! He looks like somebody who would take a color!"


**ART:** A crowd of gray creatures mid-accusation, all pointing in different directions. The last accusation lands on a completely innocent, mildly startled bystander. Visual punchline — the text never says he's innocent.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> Everybody chased everybody.

> Sivi was in the air before anyone could stop her. "WHO ARE THE DIGGERS? I'll help!"

> Bram went after Sivi. Tessa went after Bram.


**ART:** Chaos. Full-spread chase, dust, wings, everyone committed and nobody thinking. Nico must be visible somewhere in the frame doing nothing, small, easy to miss.


### SPREAD 6 (pp. 14–15)

**TEXT:**

> Nico stayed where he was.

> He crouched down.

> There was a pebble by his foot, in a puddle.

> The pebble was blue.


**ART:** Quiet spread, tight and low. Pull the whole picture down to ground level. The blue pebble in water is the only color on the page and it should stop the reader cold.


### SPREAD 7 (pp. 16–17)

**TEXT:**

> He walked. He looked.

> A red leaf, in a trickle of water.

> A yellow bug, on a root with a drip coming off it.

> "Come here," said Nico. Nobody heard him.

> "COME HERE."


**ART:** Three small discoveries staged across the spread, each one a spot of color in a gray world. Final beat: Nico shouting, which he has never done. Everyone in the background skidding to a stop.


### SPREAD 8 (pp. 18–19)

**TEXT:**

> He showed them the pebble and the leaf and the bug.

> "It's only where the water moves."

> Tessa put her hands flat on the ground.

> Gold light spread out underneath the gray — a bright vein running away up the hill, and stopping.


**ART:** Tessa's REVEAL. Lavender-white and gold. Show the glowing vein beneath the forest floor, and show clearly where it stops. The stopping place is the destination; the text doesn't need to say so.


### SPREAD 9 (pp. 20–21)

**TEXT:**

> At the top of the hill, half buried, was something somebody had built.

> Stone wheels. Wooden gates. Roots grown straight through the middle of it, holding it together.

> It was still trying to work. They could hear it trying.


**ART:** Second wonder spread — the franchise's first look at magic and engineering as one thing. Ancient, mossy, half-organic, obviously made by hands and obviously alive. Something in it should be visibly straining.


### SPREAD 10 (pp. 22–23)

**TEXT:**

> Sivi flew up into the roots. "It's stuffed! There's a big knot of them jammed in the top!"

> Nico turned a wheel.

> Nothing happened.

> He turned it back.

> He thought about it for a while. Then he turned a different one.


**ART:** Sivi tiny and high in the root canopy; Nico at the base with both hands on a stone wheel. Canon-critical: show him getting it wrong first. His face on the wrong turn and his face on the right one are two different pictures.


### SPREAD 11 (pp. 24–25)

**TEXT:**

> The gate shuddered open and all the water behind it came at once.

> Bram threw out his amber light and everybody ducked behind it.

> "Now," said Nico — and he went still, and the green light came, and the gate held long enough for Sivi to haul the roots free.


**ART:** Three powers, staged in one motion: Bram's amber shield across the foreground, Nico small and absolutely motionless at the wheel, Sivi hauling the root knot loose above. Nico is the calmest thing in a very loud picture.


### SPREAD 12 (pp. 26–27)

**TEXT:**

> The water went through.

> Then it kept going — down the hill, into the ground, into everything.


**ART:** Almost no text. Give the whole spread to water moving through the old channels and disappearing into the earth. Still gray. The reader should be leaning forward.


### SPREAD 13 (pp. 28–29)

**TEXT:**

> One fern went green at the tip.

> Just the tip.

> Just one.


**ART:** The smallest possible image on the biggest possible page. One fern frond, one green tip, acres of gray around it. Hold everything back. Page turn.


### SPREAD 14 (pp. 30–31)

**TEXT:**

> And then it went everywhere.

> Up the roots and up the trunks and out along every branch, into the ferns and the flowers and the feathers and the fur, until the birds were scarlet and the moss was gold and the whole enormous forest was so loud with color that nobody could say anything at all.

> The sky went last.


**ART:** The payoff spread and the most saturated image in the series so far. Color arriving like a flood with a visible front edge. Keep the sky pale until the final beat — it should read as the last thing to turn.


### PAGE 32 — CLOSER

**TEXT:**

> Before they left, Nico went back to the old machine.

> There was a mark cut into the stone. It was the same kind of mark that was on their packs.

> It wasn't a bear. It wasn't a unicorn. It wasn't a turtle or a butterfly.

> He put his finger on it.

> Zinumi stopped moving.

> Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

> Nobody said anything about the mark.

> **END OF BOOK 3 MANUSCRIPT**


**ART:** The mythology breadcrumb. The mark must be clearly the same *design language* as the four pack emblems and clearly none of them. Do not explain it visually either — no glow, no reaction from the stone. Zinumi's stillness is the only signal, and it should be easy to miss on a first read and impossible to miss on a second.

# BOOK 4 — The Moon Fox Who Lost His Way

**Spotlight:** Bram · **Emotional core:** sometimes helping means staying beside someone while somebody else finds the answer · **675 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> It was nearly dark, and they were walking back toward the tables, and they were arguing about the moon.

> "It's following me," said Sivi.

> "It's following everybody," said Nico.

> "That's what it wants you to think," said Sivi.

> "It followed me into the bathroom once," said Tessa.


**ART:** The park at dusk, low blue light, four children walking and looking up. Warm lantern light at the picnic tables further along the path, families packing up — small, distant, no faces. The moon large and low over the trees. A relationship beat and a piece of weather — it sets the book's palette and predicts nothing about Bram. Tessa gets the last word and is entirely serious about it.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> "Yours," said Nico.

> Only one hollow was lit, throwing a low warm shadow up the wall.

> Bram put his hand out, and it went warm, the way it had the very first time.


**ART:** Bram's bear pack lit alone in its niche, the other three dark. Keep the light low and close — this is a nighttime book and the palette should start cooling here.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> On the wall was a line of light, running away across dark hills.

> There was a wide dark band lying across the middle of it.


**ART:** Ritual line carries the prep; art does the dressing. Right-hand page: the clue. A bright road drawn across black hills with a broad band of darkness lying over the middle of it. Canon-critical: the road must read as *covered*, not *broken* — from this distance it looks like a gap, and it is not one. Do not hint that it is a shadow. Nothing else on the page.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> Everything was silver.

> Moonlight lay across the hills in long bright roads — over the grass, over the water, up and over the ridges and away.

> You could walk on them. Sivi checked.

> Zinumi lay down on one.


**ART:** The wonder spread. Silver-blue night, enormous and quiet. Moon-paths visible as solid ribbons of light crossing hills and lying flat on water. Sivi hovering one inch above one of them with a toe down, testing. Zinumi flat on her back on a moon-road with her hands behind her head — ordinary, comfortable, nothing mysterious about her at all.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> Under a glowing tree, something was crying.

> It was small. It was silver. It had a tail like a comet.

> And it would not stop looking at one particular hill.


**ART:** Baby-schema close-up — oversized ears, enormous eyes, tiny feet. The four children crouched around it at a careful distance. Canon-critical staging: the fox's eyeline must clearly point off toward a distant hill, because the text never explains why.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> The moon-road went up that hill, and then it stopped.

# BOOK 9 — The Door Beneath the Glowing Waterfall

**Spotlight:** Nico · **Emotional core:** a small problem is worth paying attention to before it becomes a big one · **690 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> There was a puddle at the bottom of the park.

> Nico had put a stone at the edge of it last Saturday, and another one the Saturday before that.

> Today he put down a third.

> "It's getting smaller," he said.

> "It's a *puddle*," said Sivi.


**ART:** Ordinary daylight, ordinary park, unremarkable puddle. Three small stones in a short line on the mud, each one further in than the last. Sivi already walking away. Families and picnic blankets visible up the slope behind them, small and out of focus. Nobody thinks this means anything.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> They were still arguing about the puddle when they saw the light.


**ART:** Nico's turtle pack lit alone in the chamber, seen from the tunnel. Four children stopped mid-argument in the doorway. Give most of the spread to the picture.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> On the wall was a valley.

> It was a nice valley. There was nothing wrong with it at all.

> Everybody looked at it for a while.


**ART:** Ritual line carries the prep. Right-hand page: the clue, and the clue is a completely pleasant landscape. Four children staring at a picture of nothing being wrong.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> It was even nicer when they got there.

> A waterfall came down the cliff in a wide shining curtain, and the river went off through the middle of everything, and the plants along it had light inside them, and everybody who lived there was having an extremely normal afternoon.


**ART:** The wonder spread, and its job is to be peaceful. Glowing waterfall, clear water, luminous plants, creatures doing chores and lounging. Beautiful and completely unalarming. The reader should be looking for the problem and not finding it.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> "Is anything broken?" said Bram.

> "Nope," said somebody carrying a basket.

> "River's a little low again," said somebody else, and yawned, and went indoors.


**ART:** Villagers being unbothered in three different ways. On the riverbank, a wet stripe of stone above the waterline — visible, and nobody looking at it.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> Nico put a stone at the edge of the water.

> Then they waited.

> Sivi lay on her back and complained about waiting.

> Tessa watched a snail cross a leaf. It took the whole time.

> "It's a snail," said Sivi.

> "Yes," said Tessa.


**ART:** One small stone, one waterline, four children waiting. Sivi upside down and vocal; Tessa on her front an inch from a snail, entirely content. This is the least eventful spread in the book and it must not look like a mistake — hold the frame, and let the two girls be opposite ways of being still. Canon-critical: Tessa must not be looking anywhere near the water. The observation belongs to Nico.


### SPREAD 6 (pp. 14–15)

**TEXT:**

> The water went down past the stone.

> Nico put down another one, and they waited again, and it went down past that one too. The same little bit. The same waiting.

> "It keeps losing the same amount," said Nico. "Something's *taking* it."


**ART:** Repeat Spread 5's composition exactly, twice, so the reader sees the change instead of being told it. Three stones now, in a short line — same picture as the puddle in the opener, at a hundred times the scale.


### SPREAD 7 (pp. 16–17)

**TEXT:**

> They went upriver, all the way to the waterfall.

> And Zinumi, who never stopped moving, stopped moving.

> She put one hand flat against the wet rock, and made a sound none of them had ever heard her make before.


**ART:** Canon-critical restraint. Zinumi absolutely still against a cliff behind falling water — no words, no symbols in the air, no explanation. The children watching her, not the rock. Her glow should shift in a way the reader can't interpret.


### SPREAD 8 (pp. 18–19)

**TEXT:**

> Tessa put her hands on the rock beside Zinumi's.

> The gold light went out across it, and out, and out, and did not stop.


**ART:** REVEAL spreading across wet stone and running off both edges of the spread. Do not show what it's outlining. Page turn.


### SPREAD 9 (pp. 20–21)

**TEXT:**

> It was a door.

> Taller than the waterfall. Older than the trees.

> And open, just a crack.

> Somebody had gone through it a long time ago and hadn't shut it.


**ART:** The reveal. An enormous ancient door behind the curtain of falling water, four children the size of pebbles in front of it, and a gap down one edge just wide enough to walk through. The crack is the whole mythology plant — somebody came before. Make it readable at a glance.


### SPREAD 10 (pp. 22–23)

**TEXT:**

> Inside, behind the water, there was a whole room of channels cut into the floor, and gates, and old gears, and roots grown down through the ceiling, and light running along the carvings in the walls.

> Most of the river was going where it always went.

> A little bit of it was going somewhere else.


**ART:** The ancient chamber. Stone channels, valves, mechanisms, roots and magical light all as one system — no comment, no wonder-struck dialogue. The wrong-way channel should be findable but not obvious; let the reader hunt for it.


### SPREAD 11 (pp. 24–25)

**TEXT:**

> Nico followed the wrong channel until it went under a wall, and lost it, and came back, and tried a different one, and got that wrong too.

> Sivi went up to the high ledge to look.

> Down at the far end, a dry stone basin was almost full.


**ART:** Nico working badly and refusing to stop — crouched, wet, backtracking, wrong. Canon-critical: this is not genius theater. Sivi small and high up, peering over a ledge at a basin nobody knew was there.


### SPREAD 12 (pp. 26–27)

**TEXT:**

> So they didn't close it. They turned it.

> It moved.


**ART:** Bram braced under a slipping stone slab, Nico motionless with both hands on an ancient lever, green-gold field steady around it. Water climbing at their feet. Effortful, never frightening.


### SPREAD 13 (pp. 28–29)

> Outside, along the whole valley, the river came up over the pale stripe of stone and kept going, and somebody dropped a basket, and everybody who had been having an extremely normal afternoon came running down to the bank to watch it fill.


**ART:** The payoff. River rising visibly along its banks, the dry stripe disappearing, the entire village pouring out of doors. Joy, motion, water. This is the emotional peak of the book — the mythology has not happened yet.


### SPREAD 14 (pp. 30–31)

**TEXT:**

**ART:** The mythology spread, and it must stay cold. A long carved wall lighting up: the four familiar emblems, and beyond them many more in the same design language, running off the edge of the page. **Do not make the number countable.** Three children staring at the wall; Nico turned the other way, watching Zinumi. No reaction shot, no glow flare, no gesture from her — just recognition, and no explanation of any kind.

# BOOK 10 — The City Beneath the Giant Leaf

**Spotlight:** Bram — first-wave finale · **Emotional core:** being strong doesn't mean doing it all yourself · **643 words**


### PAGE 3 — OPENER

**TEXT:**

> Bram had the basket, and the blanket, and both of Tessa's shoes, and a stick that Nico wanted to keep.

> "I can take something," said Tessa.

> "I got it," said Bram.


**ART:** The park path, ordinary daylight. Bram loaded up and completely content — this is not a burden he resents, it's a thing he likes being. Tessa's hand out, unneeded. The basket and blanket clearly belong to a family outing happening further up the path, out of frame. Nobody thinks anything of it.


### SPREAD 1 (pp. 4–5)

**TEXT:**

> Bram put everything down in a neat pile first.

> Then he went and got the brown pack, which had been glowing since before they arrived.


**ART:** Bear pack lit in its niche. Bram setting the basket, the blanket, both shoes and the stick down in a neat pile before he'll touch it. Small character joke, entirely in the picture.


### SPREAD 2 (pp. 6–7)

**TEXT:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> On the wall was a leaf.

> Just a leaf.


**ART:** Ritual line carries the prep. Right-hand page: one ordinary leaf on bare stone. Give it the whole page and no context whatsoever.


### SPREAD 3 (pp. 8–9)

**TEXT:**

> They came out somewhere green and enormous.

> Trees went up all around them, except they weren't trees, they were grass. There was a boulder made of glass with the whole sky bent inside it, except it was a drop of water.

> Zinumi, who was usually the smallest, was suddenly the biggest, and extremely pleased about it.


**ART:** The reveal, and the art owns it. Extreme low angle in a jungle of grass blades, a dew drop the size of a house, everything ordinary made colossal. The children's faces working it out. Zinumi hovering above them at what is now considerable size, delighted.


### SPREAD 4 (pp. 10–11)

**TEXT:**

> Something walked past them on six legs, taller than Bram, and did not look down.

> "Oh," said Nico.


**ART:** A beetle crossing the spread, enormous and utterly indifferent. Four tiny children pressed against a stem, watching it go. No fear — this realm is not dangerous, only very large.


### SPREAD 5 (pp. 12–13)

**TEXT:**

> Down among the roots there was a city.

> Bark walls and seed-shell roofs, rope bridges spun out of fiber, chimneys made of hollow stems, everything tucked into the spaces between stones — and hundreds of people no taller than the children's knees, all standing about looking worried.


**ART:** Invent this culture from scratch. Materials: bark, seed shells, plant fiber, pebbles, stems. Silhouettes, clothing, and architecture must be original — deliberately avoid mushroom-village or little-blue-folk shorthand. Their faces are the only thing wrong in a beautiful picture.


### SPREAD 6 (pp. 14–15)

**TEXT:**

> Their water came down a stone channel from somewhere higher up.

> A leaf had come off a tree and fallen across it.

> Somewhere behind the leaf, the water was still coming. In the city, the channel was dry.


**ART:** The obstruction at full scale — a single fallen leaf, ordinary from a human viewpoint, monumental from here. Show the dry channel running away into the city and the wet stain where the water stops.


### SPREAD 7 (pp. 16–17)

**TEXT:**

> "I got it," said Bram.

> He got underneath it and he pushed, and the amber came out all around him and went right up into the leaf.


**ART:** Bram's HOLD used as raw effort — light spreading up through the underside of the leaf, his back against it, feet dug in. He looks strong and capable. This must not read as comic.


### SPREAD 8 (pp. 18–19)

**TEXT:**

> It went up about as far as a finger.

> Then it came back down.

> He did it again, and it went up about as far as a finger, and came back down.

> "But this is the part I do," said Bram.


**ART:** Two identical attempts staged side by side, same composition, same result — the reader should feel the failure repeat. Then Bram sitting in the dirt, not defeated so much as confused about what he's for.


### SPREAD 9 (pp. 20–21)

**TEXT:**

> "It doesn't have to go up," said Nico.

> Tessa was already putting her hands on the ground. Gold light came up through the roots, and there it was — the stem end of the leaf, hooked under a root and holding the whole thing down like a thumb on a page.


**ART:** Canon-critical: Tessa starts before Nico finishes his sentence. She knows where he's going. The REVEAL shows the hidden catch point clearly enough that a four-year-old can see why pushing up would never work.


### SPREAD 10 (pp. 22–23)

**TEXT:**

> After that nobody argued about anything.

> Nico found a stem as long as three houses. Sivi took the vine. Two hundred people from the city took the other end of it. Bram went where Nico pointed and put both hands on the stone and didn't ask why.


**ART:** The plan assembling fast and without friction — the first-wave payoff, shown entirely through behavior. Lever, vine, crowd, anchor. Everybody already moving. Do not put a planning huddle in this picture.


### SPREAD 11 (pp. 24–25)

**TEXT:**

> Sivi went up the grass and out along a thread of silk with the vine over her shoulder, all the way across the top of the leaf, and down the other side.

> She did not do one single loop on the way.


**ART:** The adventure spread. Vertiginous scale — a small child crossing a silk strand a very long way up, dragging a line. Exhilarating, not frightening.


### SPREAD 12 (pp. 26–27)

**TEXT:**

> Then everybody pulled.

> The stem bent. The root creaked. The whole leaf started to turn, slowly, like a lid.

> And underneath all of it, Bram held the one stone that was not allowed to move, and it did not move.


**ART:** The climax composition. Vine, lever, crowd hauling, leaf rotating — and at the pivot, one boy braced against one stone with amber light around him, still and central while everything else is in motion. Without him the picture collapses.


### SPREAD 13 (pp. 28–29)

**TEXT:**

> Water found a way underneath.

> Just a thread of it, at first.

> Then the whole leaf went up and over and away, and the water came down the channel all at once.


**ART:** The payoff, and it happens *before* the turn — the only book in the wave that spends its spectacle early. Leaf lifting clear, water bursting through, ropes going slack. Page turn.


### SPREAD 14 (pp. 30–31)

**TEXT:**

> It went through the middle of the city, under the bridges, into every dry stone basin, and everybody ran alongside it shouting.

> Bram stayed exactly where he was, with his eyes shut and both hands on the stone.

> He was still there a long time after everybody else had let go, until Sivi landed on his head and told him he could stop now.


**ART:** Aftermath, not spectacle — the water has already arrived. A tiny city full and running and celebrating in the background, softer and further off. Foreground and large: one boy still braced against a stone nobody needs held anymore, eyes shut, with Sivi sitting on his head. This is the last image of the first wave and it belongs to him, not to the water.


### PAGE 32 — CLOSER

**TEXT:**

> Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

> Bram looked at the four hollows in the stone, and saw that the carved line kept going — past all of them, off into the dark further than anybody could see.

> Outside, Sivi took half the basket without asking.

> Bram let her.

> **END OF BOOK 10 MANUSCRIPT — END OF FIRST WAVE**


**ART:** Return ritual — all four packs already back in their hollows before this beat; Bram is looking, not stowing. Middle beat: the four occupied niches, and the carved pattern running on past them into unlit stone. Nothing glows, nothing activates, Zinumi has no reaction. Final image is the four of them walking out into the park in ordinary clothes, load split between them.
