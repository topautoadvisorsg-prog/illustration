### 2. THE PACK OBJECT RULE — NEW, LOCKED

This replaces the vaguer v0.14 language about dimensional storage.

#### What the pack is

The Trinkadoos Pack is the source and anchor of **one core magical verb** for its chosen child:

**Bram HOLD · Tessa REVEAL · Nico STEADY · Sivi LIFT**

That is the only thing "the pack is the source" means. It is a statement about where the *child's* power comes from.

#### What comes out of the pack

**Objects retrieved from a pack may be fully magical in their own right.** A ring, a charm, a key, a horn, a lantern, a seed, a relic — any of these can carry real power, do things none of the four children can do, and drive an entire book.

Objects are not limited by the child's verb. A ring pulled from Tessa's pack does not have to reveal anything.

#### The one constraint — PLANTED, NOT PRODUCED

If an object solves or significantly changes a problem, the reader must already have a reason it was in the pack. One of:

1. **Permanent kit** — established as always in there.
2. **Deliberately packed** — we saw the child put it in.
3. **Earned or found earlier** — from a previous book, and we saw it stored.
4. **Placed by someone** — Zinumi, the Hidden Guide, or another character, on-page.
5. **The pack itself opened something new** — a compartment, a layer, a hidden pocket. **This is a plot, not a solution.** If a pack reveals something new, that discovery becomes the story's problem or mystery. It never arrives just in time to fix the current one.

   **Edge case — two different things, don't confuse them:**
   - **A child notices a pocket that was always there.** Ordinary. No magic occurred. The pack didn't change; the child did. Allowed freely, needs no setup. *Book 2: Sivi finds an inner pocket she hadn't noticed before.* This is the permitted version.
   - **The pack opens or grows a compartment that did not exist.** Magic occurred. This is the restricted version, and rule 5 applies in full — it becomes the book's mystery, never its solution.

   The test is whether the pack changed. If the pocket was always there and the child simply hadn't looked, it is a character moment, not a magical event.

#### Why this rule exists

Not to limit magic. To keep danger real. If the pack can produce whatever is needed the moment it is needed, no problem in any book is ever actually dangerous, because the fix is always one reach away. Young children feel that.

**Practical writing standard:** plant significant objects one to two books before they matter.

#### What this does NOT restrict

- how powerful an object can be
- whether an object is magical
- rings, which are fully available again as charms, relics, keys, or plot artifacts — they are simply no longer the children's power source
- how strange, ancient, or unexplained an object is

### THE FOUR VOICES

**Bram** — short, sincere, warm. Says the obvious kind thing nobody else says. Grips his satchel strap when unsure. Never sounds like a guardian or a grown-up.
> "You're scared. It's okay to be scared."

**Tessa** — asks the sideways question. Names things. Notices small beautiful details. Can be charmingly wrong.
> "Zinumi." / "What if it's not broken? What if it's shy?"

**Nico** — fewest words of anyone. Pauses. States a fact rather than a conclusion. Never explains a mechanism at length.
> "Look. It's torn." / "It's deeper than it is."

**Sivi** — fastest, loudest, first. Talks in exclamations. Often wrong first and right second.
> "It's broken!" / "Then we're going."

**Zinumi** — chimes, trills, hums, glow changes, gestures, floating symbols, brief images. **Zero words, ever.** Her alarm reads through posture and dimmed glow. She can be wrong. She never explains the plot.

### CRAFT RULES — LOCKED

#### 1. Text and illustration divide the work

For every spread, three questions:

- What must the **text** tell us?
- What should the **illustration** tell us instead?
- Is the prose just describing what the picture already shows?

If text and art are doing identical work, cut the prose or redirect it to something only words can carry — how long something has been there, what somebody is thinking, what happened before, a sound, a fact nobody can see.

Ways a spread can divide the work well:

- visual punchline
- visual reveal
- emotion in the character's face instead of named in narration
- a picture that complicates what a child just said out loud
- scale and wonder carried almost entirely by art
- a reveal deliberately withheld until after the page turn

**Not every spread needs text and art to contradict each other.** Complement, escalation, omission, and contrast all count. This is a division of labor, not a trick to perform fourteen times a book.

#### 2. Restrained, not cold

Spare prose can still be warm. Protect:

- warmth and affection between the four children
- childlike humor
- wonder
- cozy return beats
- plain emotional clarity when the reader actually needs it

Restraint means not explaining what the art shows. It does not mean withholding feeling.

#### 3. Repetition as ritual, not formula

Recurring cadence lives in exactly three places:

- packs awakening
- getting ready and crossing
- coming home, packs back at rest

**The ritual lines:**

> Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

> Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

Short. Recognizable. Slightly variable when a book needs it. **No long chant, ever.** Nothing else repeats verbatim across books — not the power order, not the joke structure, not the climax rhythm.

The reader should think *I know this ritual*, never *I already read this book*.

#### 4. Page turns are structure

Put surprises, reveals, arrivals in new realms, creature reveals, reversals, and magic payoffs **after** the turn. Never spend a reveal in the last sentence before it. End a spread on the question; open the next one with the answer.

#### 5. Word counts are guardrails

Book 1: 700–1,000, preferred 750–900. Books 2–10: 450–700.

Never pad to reach a floor and never cut a needed beat to reach a ceiling. Word count is a symptom of pacing, not a target. If a spread-by-spread review finds a moment that genuinely needs more story, add it then — for the story, never for the number.

#### 6. Read-aloud QA

Read every manuscript out loud before handoff. Flag and fix:

- tongue-twisting phrasing
- dialogue that sounds like an adult
- monotonous sentence rhythm
- explanatory narration the art makes unnecessary
- spreads that all open the same way

Vary sentence length deliberately. Use sound, pause, and short punch lines on purpose.

---

### SENTENCE RULES

- Simple past, third person, U.S. English.
- Short sentences. One idea each. Read every line aloud.
- Dialogue-forward. If a spread has no dialogue, check whether it should.
- Never narrate an emotion the picture can show. "Bram was worried" → cut, and let the ART note handle it.
- No adult vocabulary for its own sake. No sarcasm. No teenage humor. No action-hero lines.
- Concrete nouns beat abstract ones. "The roots were torn" beats "the damage was extensive."
- Sound is allowed to carry weight — repetition, rhythm, a three-beat build. But **no rhyme.**

---

### STAGING THE POWERS

**Bram HOLD** — protects, braces, catches, shields. Amber-gold. Strongest when protecting someone, not proving something. Never a weapon.
**Tessa REVEAL** — shows what's hidden. Lavender-white and gold. **Reveals without explaining** — that gap is usually the plot.
**Nico STEADY** — local, brief stillness field. Moss green and gold. He must be still first. Not time control.
**Sivi LIFT** — glide, short flight, gentle gusts, reach high places, carry light things. Violet-blue. Carrying another child is very hard and stays rare.

**Standing series rule — no power checklist.** Not every founding power appears in every book. A child contributes through personality, observation, courage, empathy, movement, tinkering, humor, or plain problem solving just as legitimately as through magic. Never manufacture a power beat to complete a set. Established: STEADY sits out entirely in Book 7.

**Rules I hold to:**
- Not all four powers in every climax.
- Never the same order twice.
- At least one book where the emotional climax uses no magic at all (Book 1, Spread 13 — do this again).
- The spotlight child's personality is as often the **obstacle** as the solution (Books 2, 7, 10 are built on this).
- Every child matters in every book, but not through magic every time — observation, courage, empathy, staying put, tinkering, a mistake they own.

---

### HARD NOS

- No sermon ending. No "and that's how they learned…"
- No new permanent powers, ever.
- No object out of a pack that wasn't planted (see tracker).
- No ordinary human adults in the Adventure Realms.
- No competent authority figure who makes the children pointless.
- No child left behind — they always return together.
- No gore, horror, realistic violence, or nihilism. **Wonder is stronger than fear.**
- No villain by default. Misunderstanding, fear, weather, accident, broken system, someone needing help.
- **When characters blame the wrong thing, keep it child-scale and a little funny** — confused, hasty, absurd. Never hostile, never a mob, and never followed by anyone explaining why blaming is bad. The book corrects it by discovering the real cause, not by making a speech.
- No lore dump. Mythology arrives as an image nobody explains.

---

### ART NOTE DISCIPLINE

An ART note gets written only for:
- information the illustration must establish
- a visual reveal
- a canon-critical detail (dimensional interior, physically dressing, packs at rest)
- a page-turn payoff

Not for background, mood, or restating the prose. If the note repeats the text, cut the note.

---

### THE ONE-LINE TEST PER BOOK

Before I hand a draft over, it has to pass all five:

1. Is there **one clear hook** a four-year-old would want on the cover?
2. Is there **one payoff spread** that justifies the book existing?
3. Does the spotlight child's **personality** decide the outcome — not their power?
4. Does the world end **brighter than it began**?
5. Would this still be a good story if you deleted the lesson?

### KEEPSAKE STAGING — VARY IT

Keepsakes are now a real recurring system. The system repeats; **the beat must not.**

Used so far:
- Book 2 — *given* by the creature, stored on the closing page
- Book 4 — *pushed into a hand* at the last second, stored on the closing page

Both were end-of-book handovers. Do not do a third. Future options: an object *found* mid-adventure and pocketed without comment; something discovered in the pack a book *later*; something attached to the outside of a pack rather than stored inside; a book with no keepsake at all. Book 5 deliberately has none.

The continuity should read as intentional, never mechanical.

### STANDING CHECKS BEFORE ANY OBJECT COMES OUT OF A PACK

1. Is it on this tracker?
2. If no — does the manuscript show it going in, earlier in this same book?
3. If no — does someone put it in on-page?
4. If no — **change the scene.**

A pack opening to reveal something new is allowed and good. It just becomes the book's mystery, never the book's solution.

# PART SEVEN — PRINT & PUBLISHING SPEC

Researched August 2026. Verify live figures before committing spend.

## Locked format

| Format | Platform | Trim | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paperback | Amazon KDP | 8.5 x 8.5 | 32 |
| Kindle | Amazon KDP | — | — |
| Hardcover | IngramSpark | 8.5 x 8.5, case laminate | 32 |

**All square. One interior art layout serves all formats.** Separate cover files required for paperback and hardcover (hardcover wraps boards and needs more bleed).

## Why hardcover is not on KDP

KDP hardcover requires a **75-page minimum** and offers **no square trim size** — confirmed still current as of June 2026. A 32-page square hardcover cannot be produced there at any price. IngramSpark case hardcover starts at 24 pages and supports 8.5 x 8.5.

The Ingram hardcover still sells on Amazon: Ingram's catalog feeds Amazon automatically. Amazon lists and sells it; Ingram prints and ships it. Not Prime, thinner margin, but present on the same storefront.

## Non-negotiables before art or setup

1. **Buy publisher-owned ISBNs from Bowker.** One per format per book — 10 books x 3 formats = 30 ISBNs. A KDP-assigned ISBN is owned by Amazon and cannot transfer to Ingram.
2. **Turn Expanded Distribution OFF on KDP.** It drops royalty from 60% to 40% and collides with Ingram.
3. **Same ISBN across KDP and Ingram** for a given format, so Amazon shows one product page rather than competing listings.
4. **Order proof copies in standard color AND premium color** before committing ten books to an ink tier.

## Money mechanics

**KDP:** royalty = (rate x list price) − print cost. Rate is **60% at or above $9.99**, **50% below** (changed June 10, 2025; applies to paperback and hardcover).

**Ingram:** compensation = list price − wholesale discount − print cost − market access fee (**1.875%**). The wholesale discount (40–55%, publisher-set) goes to the *retailer*, not Ingram. 40% for online-focused; 55% + returnable is what bookstores and libraries expect.

## Print cost notes

- Standard color vs premium color is roughly a **3x** difference. One real-world data point: a 48-page 8.5-inch square children's book printed at **$4.10 standard color**; the same book in premium color at a different trim ran **$11.16**.
- Premium color suits full-bleed illustration with heavy ink coverage; **70lb / 104gsm white** paper is the stock that supports all color options.
- KDP charges a **flat rate for premium color books of 24–40 pages** ($3.60 at regular trim on Amazon.com) instead of per-page. 8.5 x 8.5 is "large" trim so the rate will be higher, but short color paperbacks price favorably.
- Consider **standard color on the paperback, premium on the hardcover.** The formats do not have to match.

## Open verification items

- Confirm **8.5 x 8.5 hardcover** availability in the live IngramSpark dashboard — the entire plan depends on it, and only secondhand sources confirm it.
- Confirm whether 8.5 x 8.5 Ingram hardcover offers a **dust jacket** or **case laminate only** — affects the cover design brief.
- Confirm current **IngramSpark title setup fee**. Sources conflict: some report setup is now free, others still quote $49 print / $25 ebook. Promo codes waiving fees circulate through ALLi and IBPA.
- Pull real print costs from the **KDP calculator** and **Ingram Print & Ship calculator** at 8.5 x 8.5 / 32 pages / premium color before setting any list price.

## Longer-term

Print-on-demand color hardcover is expensive per unit. If the series sells, **offset printing** in runs of 2,000+ is where the margin is on a ten-book franchise. POD is the proving ground, not the endgame.

A **3-in-1 hardcover omnibus** (96 pages) would clear KDP's 75-page minimum, but only in a portrait trim — requiring a second art layout. Optional gift SKU, not a substitute for single-title hardcovers, which is what libraries and schools buy. Deferred.
