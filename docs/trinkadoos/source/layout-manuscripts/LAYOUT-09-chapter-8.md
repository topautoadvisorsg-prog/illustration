# CHAPTER 8
## The Creature Who Didn't Want to Be Seen

"Nice horse," said Bram.

"It isn't a horse."

Tessa explained what it was.

"Okay," said Bram. "Nice horse."

Tessa explained it again, louder and with her hands.

Something was lit in the next room, and it was hers.

Tessa went past it still explaining.

Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

On the wall was a spoon. And a button. And a short piece of blue string.

That was all.

The village was built into the side of a hill, all round doors and crooked chimneys, and everybody in it was outside arguing.

Things had been going missing.

A ladle. Nine buttons. Somebody's second-best hat.

Zinumi copied everybody's pointing until Tessa made her stop.

"It has enormous teeth," said somebody.

"It has *twelve feet*," said somebody else.

"It has a tail longer than the bridge and claws you can't see and it can go through a wall."

Tessa went and looked instead.

She put her hands down by an empty hook, and gold light came up — a thread caught on a nail, and small flat footprints going off between two houses, and stopping in the middle of nothing.

"They don't stop," said Nico. "They go up."

Sivi went up too, and found a gap behind the chimney stones that nobody had ever needed to look at.

Inside, in the warm dark behind the chimney, was a nest.

It was made of nine buttons and a ladle and somebody's second-best hat and a hundred pieces of string, all tucked and folded and put in order.

And in the middle of it was something about the size of Tessa's shoe, with enormous ears, holding very still.

"We have to show everybody!" said Sivi. "They think you've got twelve feet!"

The creature went flat against the wall of its nest and did not move at all.

"But if they could just see you—" said Tessa.

Then she shut her mouth.

"Okay," she said. "Then they won't."

So they took everything back without it.

Nico worked out whose was whose from what had been taken together. Bram carried the ladle. Sivi did the roofs.

By morning every doorstep in the village had its things on it, and nobody had any idea how.

It should have finished there.

But the village still had a monster with twelve feet in it, and now the monster was *polite*, which everybody found much worse.

Then, out past the bridge, the fog came.

Not ordinary fog. It glowed, and it hummed, and it came over the fields toward the hill without any wind behind it at all.

Nobody in the village noticed.

Behind the chimney stones, two enormous ears went up.

The sound it made was terrible.

It was long and high and wobbly and it went right through everybody's head, and the whole village turned around with their hands over their ears —

and saw the fog.

They ran, and Bram got in front of the fog and held it off the path with both arms and all the amber he had, and the whole village came up the hill hand in hand and in through the round doors.

Afterward everybody stood in the square looking up at the chimney.

And after a while, on its own, it came out.

Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

Bram looked at the drawing on the wall again.

"I still think it's a horse," he said.

"Okay," said Tessa.
