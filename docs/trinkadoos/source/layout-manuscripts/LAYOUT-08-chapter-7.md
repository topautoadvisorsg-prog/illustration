# CHAPTER 7
## The Firefly Festival That Lost Its Spark

Clap. Clap. Clap-clap-clap.

Clap. Clap. Clap-cl— "SIVI."

"What?"

"You keep going early."

"I know what comes next!" said Sivi.

The purple pack was glowing so hard it had lit up the whole doorway.

Sivi was through it before anybody else had stood up.

Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

Sivi didn't even look at the wall.

She went straight through — and came out into the loudest, brightest night any of them had ever seen.

The rest of them came through after her.

The festival was already going.

Boats with lanterns on them. Fruit floating in the air on strings. Drums, and something like a fiddle, and somebody selling something hot in a leaf. Creatures dancing on the bank and creatures dancing in the water and one creature dancing badly on a roof.

Zinumi went straight for whatever was in the leaf.

The fireflies were the whole reason for the party.

Every year they made the sky into pictures.

Tonight they were flying in circles going *flick*. And *flick*. And nothing.

"I've got this," said Sivi.

"Everybody go HIGHER!"

And they did, and for about two seconds a whole line of them came on together, gold and moving.

"YES! Now faster!"

The line went out.

"Bigger circles! Everybody spread out! No — closer! Do the first one again but faster!"

A boat tipped. Bram got a shoulder under it and held it while everybody climbed off.

Somebody's lanterns came down in the water.

Sivi flew higher and shouted louder.

"That nearly worked," said Nico.

"That nearly worked last time too," said Tessa.

Sivi had her mouth open for the next idea.

She closed it.

"They stop when I talk," said Sivi.

Tessa put her hands up from the bank.

All through the dark over the river, faint gold trails came out — a long looping path, going round and round and back on itself, the same shape over and over.

They had been flying it all night. Underneath everything Sivi told them.

Sivi stopped flapping so hard and listened.

There was a sound. She had been talking over it since she got there.

Wings, hundreds of them: *hush-hush-HUSH. hush-hush-HUSH.*

So she went and flew it with them.

Not in front. In the middle, where nobody could see her, going round the long loop the way they went round it — *hush-hush-HUSH* — and not saying one single word.

Beside her, one firefly came on and stayed on.

Then six. Then a whole cluster, low over the water.

Down on the bank the drummer heard it, and stopped playing his song, and started playing theirs.

And the whole sky came on.

Hundreds of them, all together, sweeping over the river in one enormous turning shape — gold and violet and blue, breaking apart and coming back, so bright the water underneath had a second festival in it, and every creature in the valley put its head back at the same time.

Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

Then they did the clapping game again, and Sivi came in half a beat early — and heard it, and waited, and got the very next one exactly right.

Then she got the three after that completely wrong.

"Sorry," said Sivi.

She was not sorry.
