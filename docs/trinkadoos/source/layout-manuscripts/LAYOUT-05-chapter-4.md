# CHAPTER 4
## The Moon Fox Who Lost His Way

It was nearly dark, and they were walking back toward the tables, and they were arguing about the moon.

"It's following me," said Sivi.

"It's following everybody," said Nico.

"That's what it wants you to think," said Sivi.

"It followed me into the bathroom once," said Tessa.

"Yours," said Nico.

Only one hollow was lit, throwing a low warm shadow up the wall.

Bram put his hand out, and it went warm, the way it had the very first time.

Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

On the wall was a line of light, running away across dark hills.

There was a wide dark band lying across the middle of it.

Everything was silver.

Moonlight lay across the hills in long bright roads — over the grass, over the water, up and over the ridges and away.

You could walk on them. Sivi checked.

Zinumi lay down on one.

Under a glowing tree, something was crying.

It was small. It was silver. It had a tail like a comet.

And it would not stop looking at one particular hill.

The moon-road went up that hill, and then it stopped.

There was a long piece of dark where the light should have been.

Somewhere on the other side of the dark was where the fox was supposed to be going.

Sivi was already ten feet up. "I'll go and see!"

"There'll be a reason," said Nico. "There's always a reason."

Bram wanted to go too.

He wanted to go a lot.

Then he looked down.

Every time somebody said the word *go*, the little fox got smaller.

"You three go," said Bram. "I'll stay here."

"You'll miss it," said Sivi.

"I know," said Bram.

So they went.

He sat down in the grass, and the fox sat as far away from him as it could get, which was not very far.

He could hear the others out on the hill, being excited.

He stayed where he was.

Far off, Tessa put her hands up.

And the road was still there. All of it. Running on underneath the dark, all the way over the hill.

Nico looked at the edge of the dark, and kept looking.

"That's too straight," said Nico.

"Something's making that."

Then everybody looked up.

High up on the ridge, the windstorm had packed a whole hillside of leaves into a thorn bush, and the thorn bush was standing right in front of the moon.

From down in the grass, Bram watched Sivi fly up at it and get blown back. And fly up at it and get blown back.

The third time she got behind it, and pushed the wind through instead of at it, and the leaves went up all at once like a flock of birds.

The dark slid back off the hill like a blanket coming off a bed, and the road underneath came on all at once, all the way to the edge of a black ravine.

The fox looked at it.

The fox sat down.

So Bram picked it up, and held it, and walked out onto the light with warm amber glowing all around the two of them.

The road went across, and up, and over, and onto a ridge with nothing on it.

Nothing at all.

Then one fox stood up.

Then another one. Then four. Then more than anybody could count, all along the ridge, until the whole top of the hill was silver and moving.

The fox went out of Bram's arms so fast it left a line of light behind it.

It came back once, and pushed something into his hand. A small flat stone with a white curve on it, like a piece of a moon.

All the way home Sivi told him what he'd missed. The thorn bush. The wind. The leaves going up.

Bram put the moon-stone in his pack.

Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

Then he asked her to tell it again.
