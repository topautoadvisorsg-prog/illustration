# CHAPTER 6
## The Bridge That Forgot How to Build Itself

When it rained hard, the whole cave hummed.

You couldn't hear it standing up. You had to lie down on the floor with your ear on the stone.

So that's what they were doing.

Except Sivi, who did it for a bit and then got up to find out whether it was louder over there.

"Sivi," said Nico.

"It IS louder over there!"

There was green light coming through the doorway.

There had probably been green light coming through the doorway for a while. Nobody had their eyes open except Sivi, and she was facing the wrong way.

Out came the hoods and the boots and the wings. On went the packs.

On the wall was a bridge.

All of its pieces were there. None of them were in the right place.

A fast brown river went straight through the middle of the woods.

On the near bank, a crowd of creatures stood waiting with baskets and bundles and one extremely patient goat.

Zinumi sat on the goat.

Then the ground rumbled, and the bridge began to build itself.

A railing came up and lay down flat like a floor.

A big support turned around backward.

Two vines tied a step to a step.

Somewhere in the middle, a handrail grew straight up out of nothing and just stood there.

Everybody ran at it.

"The railing! Get the railing!"

They pushed the railing back up. The bridge tied two more steps together behind them.

Nico's hand went out toward a gear.

Then he took his hand back.

"Stop," said Nico.

Nobody stopped.

"*STOP.*"

So they all stood still and let the bridge do it wrong.

The whole way through. Every piece.

Then it came apart and started again.

The railing lay down flat.

The support turned around backward.

The vines tied a step to a step.

Nico counted them off on his fingers.

"It keeps doing the same wrong thing," he said. "In the same order."

"It isn't broken," said Nico. "Somebody's telling it wrong."

Along the bank, half sunk in the mud, were little carved markers in a long row. The bridge was reading them one at a time.

One of the markers was gone.

Tessa knelt down and put her hands in the mud, and gold light came up through it — the shape of the missing one, still there underneath, facing the wrong way.

The one that said *turn around*.

The river started coming up.

Sivi went for the top vine, the one nobody could reach, and pulled it back where it belonged.

Bram got both arms around a stone that was sliding, and held it, and did not let go.

Nico got down in the mud with the marker in both hands.

The bank was shaking. The water was loud.

He went still — the way he always had to be still first — and the green light came, and the shaking stopped just around his hands, and he turned the little stone the right way round and pushed it down into the mud.

He got up. He stepped back.

Nothing happened.

Then something clicked, a long way off, under the water.

The whole bridge came alive at once.

Roots whipped tight and stones came up out of the river and slats spun into place and every marker on the bank lit up one after another after another, all the way across — and the crowd ran, and the children ran, and the goat went first, over a bridge that was still building itself under their feet.

Everybody else ran off up the far bank.

Nico stayed and watched the bridge take itself apart again — slowly, piece by piece, in exactly the right order.

Off came the hoods and the boots and the wings. Back went the packs.

"That was the good part," said Nico.
